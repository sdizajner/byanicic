01

Artificial Intelligence

AI is part of how I explore, draft and build. Here is exactly where it sits in the work — and where the judgement stays mine.

Current practice — August 2026
01 — How AI is used

AI tools sit in the same drawer as Figma, Photoshop and a code editor. I reach for them early, when the cost of an idea should be close to zero, and less and less as the work moves toward final.

  • Concept exploration and creative ideation
  • Image generation and presentation mockups
  • UI, layout and motion exploration
  • Copy drafting, editing and refinement
  • Code generation and development assistance
  • The animated build sequence in the hero, generated with Claude

None of this replaces design work. It widens the search before the decisions start.

02 — Human creative direction

Every prompt, every direction, every selection and every revision is mine. AI produces options; it does not choose between them, set the brief, or sign anything off.

Whatever is published here or delivered to a client goes through human review proportionate to the work — a concept image gets a designer's eye, production code gets read and tested.

03 — AI-assisted visual content

The presentation scenes in the portfolio — the laptop shots, rooms and lighting around each screenshot — are AI-generated from my prompts. What sits inside those scenes is not: the branding, layouts, interfaces and creative direction are drawn by hand in Figma, Illustrator and Photoshop.

The animated build sequence in the hero was generated with Claude, then reviewed, refined and integrated into the site by hand. It is code rather than imagery, and the final implementation is mine.

Elsewhere, AI-assisted editing is used where it earns its place — retouching a photograph, cleaning up an export. I would rather say all of this once, plainly, than scatter badges across the site. Ask about any specific asset and you'll get a straight answer about how it was made.

04 — Website copy

Some of the writing on byanicic.com started life as an AI draft. All of it was read, cut, rewritten where it needed to be, and editorially approved before it went live.

Voice is half the job here, so nothing goes out that I wouldn't have written myself.

05 — Concept projects

The portfolio holds both kinds of work, and each project says which it is in the line under its title.

  • Client project — commissioned, delivered, in use.
  • Concept project — self-initiated, unbriefed, built to demonstrate capability.

Concept work isn't lesser work. It's often where the sharpest ideas live, because nothing has been negotiated down. But it is not a commission, and it is never presented as one.

06 — Transparency

The EU AI Act sets specific, human-visible disclosure duties for certain kinds of synthetic content — deepfake depictions of real people among them. Where something on this site or in delivered work falls into one of those categories, the disclosure belongs on that content in its own context, not on this page.

This section describes actual practice rather than certifying compliance, and it makes no promise that provenance metadata such as C2PA survives every export, upload and platform — that is outside what this workflow can guarantee today.

02

Privacy

What this site collects, what it doesn't, and what happens to anything you send me.

Current practice — August 2026
01 — What this site collects

Nothing, directly. There is no contact form, no newsletter, no login, no analytics and no advertising or tracking script of any kind on byanicic.com. No third party is loaded into these pages, so no third party learns that you visited.

The only record of your visit is the ordinary access log my hosting provider keeps — IP address, time, page, browser — which exists to keep the site running and secure, and which I neither analyse nor combine with anything else.

02 — What is stored on your device

No cookies are set. Three small values live in your browser's own storage, and none of them identifies you:

  • preferredLang — remembers EN or SR after you use the language switch
  • bacSiteEntered — stops the intro animation replaying on every page
  • langRedirected — prevents a redirect loop between the two languages

All three are set by something you did, serve only the interface, and can be cleared at any time in your browser settings. That is why this site has no cookie banner: there is nothing here that consent law requires you to agree to.

03 — When you get in touch

Email and phone are the only channels. What you send me — your name, your address, whatever you choose to include — sits in my mailbox and becomes the basis of our working relationship. The lawful basis is my legitimate interest in answering you, and a contract once we start work.

Kept while the project is live, then as long as invoicing and tax law requires, then deleted. Nothing is sold, shared or used for marketing.

04 — Your rights

Under the GDPR you can ask what I hold about you, ask for a copy, have it corrected or erased, or object to my keeping it. One email is enough and there is no form to fill in.

I am the controller for this data: Aleksandar Aničić, Belgrade, Serbia. If you think I have handled something badly, you can complain to your national data protection authority, or to the Serbian Commissioner for Information of Public Importance and Personal Data Protection.

04

Security

How your files, credentials and site access are handled while we work together — and after.

Full text in preparation

Credentials are never sent as plain text over email or chat. Project files sit in access-controlled storage, hosting and transfers run over TLS, and my access is removed once handoff is complete.

This is a one-person studio, so here's the honest version: security is a set of disciplined habits rather than a certification programme. If something ever goes wrong, you'll hear it from me quickly and directly.

  • Credential handling and access control
  • File storage and transfer
  • Hosting, TLS and backups
  • Incident notification
05

Accessibility

The standard I build to, and what to do if something here doesn't work for you.

Full text in preparation

WCAG 2.2 level AA is the target for this site and for everything I deliver: keyboard-reachable navigation, visible focus, an honest heading structure, sufficient contrast and full respect for reduced-motion preferences.

No site is ever finished on this front. If any part of byanicic.com is hard to use with a screen reader, a keyboard or magnification, tell me — I'd rather fix it than explain it away.

  • Assistive technology support
  • Known limitations
